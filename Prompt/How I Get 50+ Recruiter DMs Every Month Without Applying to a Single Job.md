Your Headline

# Your headline is your SEO keyword field, not your job title.
Recruiters use LinkedIn Sales Navigator to find candidates. They type in a skill or role and the algorithm surfaces profiles that match. Your headline is one of the first places it looks.

## How Sales Navigator actually works
- Recruiter opens Sales Navigator and types in a role or skill
- LinkedIn scans headlines, skills, and experience sections for keyword matches
- Profiles with the right keywords appear at the top. Everyone else is invisible.

## Weak vs strong
Weak

Software Engineer at Amazon. Tells the algorithm nothing beyond your employer.

Strong

Software Engineer @ Amazon | Backend | AWS | Java | Distributed Systems | UIUC CS 2025. Hits multiple search terms at once.

## Formula
Current role and company

- 2 to 3 core technical skills
- 1 domain you specialize in
- Education or credential if relevant

### Quick Action

Rewrite your headline right now. Open LinkedIn, go to edit profile, and add at least 4 keywords. It takes 3 minutes. This is the single highest-leverage change you can make today.

Your About Section

# Your About section is your sales pitch. Treat it like one.
Once a recruiter finds your profile from a search, your About section is what makes them stop scrolling. It needs to answer three questions immediately: Who are you, what have you done, and why should I reach out right now.

## What to include

- A human opening line that is not just your job title
- The most impressive thing you have shipped or built, with a real number attached
- Your specific skills and the scale at which you have used them
- What you are open to and how to reach you

## What to avoid
- “Passionate engineer seeking new opportunities” says nothing
- Long walls of text with no white space
- Generic buzzwords like results-oriented or self-starter
- Copying from your resume verbatim

## Here is mine for reference

Experience

Most people write job descriptions. Write proof instead.
Recruiters care about results, not responsibilities. Every bullet in your experience section should have an action, a metric, and the technology you used.

The formula
Template

[Action verb] [what you built or fixed] using [technology], resulting in [specific metric or outcome].

Weak

Led a team and improved system performance.

Strong

Led 6 engineers to cut API latency by 40%, shipping 2 weeks ahead of schedule.

Keyword strategy
List every technology, language, and framework you touched in that role

Include team size, system scale, and user impact wherever you can

Recruiters search for Java, Python, AWS, Kubernetes separately. Each one you add is a new reason to show up in search.

Quick Action

Go to your most recent role and rewrite 2 bullets using the formula above. Add the name of every technology you used, even if it was minor.

Certifications

Every certification is a search filter you now pass.
Some recruiters filter candidates exclusively by certification. AWS Cloud Practitioner, Google Cloud, PMP, Scrum Master. If you have one and have not listed it, you are invisible in that search. Student and online certs count too. Coursera, edX, Postman, Meta Blueprint. All of it.

Why it matters beyond the obvious
Each cert is a filter you now pass in Sales Navigator

Certifications signal continuous learning, which hiring managers actively value

Every certification adds another signal for LinkedIn’s algorithm to surface your profile

Quick Action

Go to your LinkedIn profile, click Add Section, and add every certification you have ever completed. Aim for a minimum of 5. If you have none, complete one free LinkedIn Learning course today and add it immediately.

Activity

Active users get 10x more profile views. You just need to show up.
You do not need to go viral. You do not need to become a content creator. You need to show up consistently enough that LinkedIn’s algorithm keeps surfacing you.

Three ways to stay active without burning out
Post original content once a week. Share what you are building, learning, or figuring out. One post a week is enough.

Comment on posts in your field. A specific, thoughtful comment on a well-followed person’s post puts your profile in front of their entire audience.

Repost with your own take. Find a post you agree with, repost it, add 2 to 3 sentences about why it matters to you. Takes 90 seconds.

Content ideas if you have no idea what to post
A problem you solved at work and how you solved it

A tool you discovered that saved you time

Something you got wrong and what you learned

A career decision you made and why

Quick Action

Write one LinkedIn post today. 3 to 5 sentences about something you learned this week. Post it and leave it.

Open to Work

Never turn Open to Work on for everyone. Ever.
The green banner is visible to your current employer, your clients, and your entire network. The moment you turn it on for everyone, you signal that you are available and possibly not by choice. That is not the signal you want to send.

Do NOT do this

All LinkedIn Members. The green ring shows on your photo publicly. Your boss can see it.

Do this instead

Recruiters only. Visible only inside Sales Navigator. No green banner. Nobody else knows.

How to set it correctly
Go to your LinkedIn profile

Click Open to and select Finding a new job

Under who can see you are open, select Recruiters only

Fill in the job types and locations you actually want

Save

Quick Action

Check your Open to Work setting right now. If it is set to All LinkedIn Members, switch it to Recruiters only. Takes 60 seconds.

Featured Section

Pin your resume at the top. Make it one click to download.
The Featured section sits right below your header. It is the first thing a recruiter sees after your name and headline. Most people leave it empty. Pinning your resume here means a recruiter can download it in one click without ever having to ask you.

What to pin
Your resume as a PDF file

A portfolio link or personal website if you have one

Your best LinkedIn post if it performed well and represents your expertise

A case study, GitHub repo, or project link if relevant to the roles you want

How to add your resume in 60 seconds
Go to your LinkedIn profile

Click Add profile section

Select Featured and then Media

Upload your resume as a PDF

Set the title to Download My Resume

Save and drag it to the top of your Featured section

Quick Action

Upload your resume to your Featured section today. Recruiters spend an average of 7 seconds on a profile. Make your resume the first thing they reach.
